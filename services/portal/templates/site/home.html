For teams and self-hosters

Private administration with WireGuard

The operator console isn't on the public internet. It only accepts connections through a WireGuard tunnel, an SSH tunnel or allow-listed addresses.

  • {% icon "sparkles" %}

    Set up when you install

    WireGuard is on by default: the installer brings up the tunnel and creates a first operator device. Operators can turn it off, and if a host can't run WireGuard the console stays reachable through an SSH tunnel.

  • {% icon "key-round" %}

    A preshared key for every device

    Each peer gets its own preshared key, which keeps recorded tunnel traffic confidential even against a future quantum computer, as long as the key stays secret.

  • {% icon "sliders-horizontal" %}

    Managed from the console

    Add or revoke operator devices on the console's WireGuard page, or with lumenctl wireguard add-peer.

{% icon "info" %}WireGuard protects how administrators reach the server. It doesn't carry your chats: messages are end-to-end encrypted by Matrix on their own.

Run your own {{ brand.short_name }} server

Website, portal, homeserver, web chat, calls, backups and monitoring install on a fresh Ubuntu 24.04 server with one command. It's built on open standards, so you are never locked in.

$ git clone <your Lumen repository> lumen
$ cd lumen && sudo bash install.sh
# Checks the server, asks for your domain and email,
# issues TLS certificates, starts every service, sets up
# WireGuard for operator access and schedules backups.

$ sudo ./lumenctl wireguard add-peer laptop
# Another operator device, with its own preshared key.

Questions people ask

Can {{ brand.short_name }} staff read my messages?

Not in encrypted rooms, which is every room by default. Messages and files are encrypted on your devices with keys that never reach our servers. We can see metadata such as who is in a room and when messages are sent.

What is Matrix, and why does it matter?

Matrix is the open standard for secure, decentralised messaging. Because {{ brand.short_name }} speaks standard Matrix, you can talk to people on other Matrix servers, sign in with other Matrix apps such as Element, or move to your own server without starting over.

Is it post-quantum secure?

Not yet for message content, and we won't claim otherwise. Matrix's Olm/Megolm encryption uses classical cryptography (X25519, Ed25519, AES-256). Connections to our servers use a hybrid post-quantum key exchange (X25519MLKEM768) when your browser supports it. Our security page explains exactly what that does and doesn't protect.

Do my messages go through WireGuard?

No. WireGuard is how operators reach the private admin console. Your messages are protected by Matrix end-to-end encryption on their own, from your device to the people you talk to.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Sign in on another device and unlock your history with your recovery key, or verify the new device from another one you still have. Without either, old encrypted messages can't be recovered — by design, not even by us.

What happens to my data if I stop paying?

Nothing is deleted. Your workspace moves to the Free plan's limits after a grace period; your chats and files stay exactly where they are.
