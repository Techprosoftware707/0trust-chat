End-to-end encryption

Chats use the Matrix protocol's encryption: Olm (a Double Ratchet derivative) to set up keys between devices, and Megolm to encrypt room messages efficiently for every device in a room. The implementation is vodozemac, a Rust library maintained by the Matrix.org Foundation and audited by Least Authority, compiled to WebAssembly and used through the official Matrix JavaScript SDK. {{ brand.short_name }} does not implement any cryptography of its own.

  • New rooms are encrypted by default, server-wide.
  • Keys are generated on your device. Our servers store only public keys and ciphertext.
  • Attachments are encrypted in your browser (AES-256-CTR with a SHA-256 integrity hash) before upload.
  • Rooms that are not encrypted — for example a public room joined from another server — are clearly labelled as such.

Verification

Encryption is only as good as knowing who holds the keys. Verify a contact by comparing a short list of emoji on both screens. With cross-signing, each person has a master identity key that signs their own devices, so you verify a person once. If someone's identity key changes — because they reset their account, or because someone else is trying to impersonate them — the app warns you before you keep talking.

Backup & recovery

Your devices can upload an encrypted backup of message keys to the server. It is encrypted with a key derived from your recovery key, which is generated on your device and shown to you once. We never receive it. If you lose all your devices and your recovery key, your encrypted history cannot be recovered by anyone — including us.

Transport security

Every public endpoint is HTTPS-only with TLS 1.2+ (TLS 1.3 preferred), HSTS and strict security headers. Certificates are issued and renewed automatically. Server-to-server federation traffic is also TLS-encrypted and every federation request is signed by the sending server's key.

Post-quantum status

Large quantum computers would break the elliptic-curve cryptography that almost every messenger relies on, and an adversary could record encrypted traffic today to decrypt it later. Here is where {{ brand.short_name }} stands:

LayerStatus
Message encryption (Olm/Megolm)Not post-quantum. Matrix has no standardised, interoperable post-quantum Olm/Megolm yet. We will adopt one once it is specified, implemented upstream and reviewed — not before.
Browser ↔ server connectionsHybrid post-quantum key exchange (X25519MLKEM768, combining X25519 with NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM) is offered and used automatically when your browser supports it. This protects the connection to our server, not the end-to-end layer.
Server ↔ server federationHybrid key exchange is used when both servers support it; many Matrix servers do not yet.
Operator VPN (WireGuard)Each peer uses a preshared key, which WireGuard mixes into its handshake for post-quantum resistance of the tunnel.

You will not see a "quantum-safe" badge on your chats, because that would not be true. The full analysis is published with the source code.

Account security

  • Passwords are hashed with Argon2id. Minimum length 12, with checks against common passwords.
  • Two-factor authentication with authenticator apps, security keys and one-time recovery codes. Workspaces can require it.
  • Sign-in rate limiting, session idle timeouts and a list of active sessions you can end at any time.
  • Your chat sign-in goes through your {{ brand.short_name }} account (single sign-on). The account service decides who you are; it never sees your message keys.

Limits we want you to know

  • If your device is compromised (malware, an unlocked phone), encryption can't help: the attacker sees what you see.
  • A compromised account service or server could try to add a new device to your account. Other people will see an unverified device and a warning; keep verification on.
  • The web app is delivered by the server you use. Someone who controls that server could serve modified code. For the highest assurance, use a desktop or mobile Matrix app from an independent source.
  • {{ brand.short_name }} is not an anonymity tool. Use Tor or similar if you need to hide your IP address.
